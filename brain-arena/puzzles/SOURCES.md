# Balance, pattern and relay practice

These games use independently generated problems and the shared BRAIN ARENA interface. No video frames or third-party artwork are bundled.

## Source and rule checks

- **Balance scale:** [tvN D ENT / Diggle's Cooperative Hold'em clip](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x7lhap7) identifies the original match. The [episode's rule record](https://www.namu.moe/w/더%20지니어스:그랜드%20파이널/10화) describes using a specified number of counters to balance two sides; each numbered position gives the contribution of one counter there. The browser practice permits stacking counters and accepts every allocation meeting the stated number and equality. Distances are decorative and are not multiplied into the numbered contributions.
- **Same Pattern:** [season 1 final rule record](https://www.namu.moe/w/대학전쟁/5회전), original name `숨은 패턴 찾기`: a 10-column, 8-row arrow board; a 2×2 target; rotations permitted, reflections excluded; answer by upper-left coordinate; correct +1, incorrect/duplicate −1; a valid end declaration +3, an early end −2. Six targets use two boards, replaced before round four. Rotation changes both arrow directions and positions.
- **Time Shift and Pixel Art:** [season 3 relay rule record](https://www.namu.moe/w/대학전쟁3/3회전). The original games use number consumption and mental rotation of three of nine 3×3 colored pictures. The user's practice requirements are authoritative for this version: Time Shift supplies exactly the digits 1–6 or 1–7, once each; Pixel Art accepts three card numbers and internally checks all quarter-turn rotations, with no rotation controls. Every accepted overlay forbids repeated instances of the same primary color in one cell, including same-color-only overlap. Distinct primaries mix as orange, purple, green or black. Reflections are excluded.
- **Block Count:** [season 1 relay rule record](https://www.namu.moe/w/대학전쟁/4회전) identifies counting all cubes in the depicted stack. The new problems use solid columns and a monotone staircase, making every column's top visible. Hidden support cubes are included; empty hidden columns and floating cubes are not generated.

## Browser practice choices

The five games are solo practice. Free practice has no deadline. Challenge mode uses a clearly labeled two-minute limit per problem or pattern round; this is a browser practice setting, not a claim about the broadcast's timing or team rules. Incorrect submissions have a ten-second retry delay. Scale progresses through five levels. Time Shift, Pixel Art and Block Count each progress through eight levels; their introductory difficulty remains gentle while the upper limit increases. Clear advances, forfeit reveals a valid answer and retains the difficulty for the next fresh problem. Pattern uses six rounds and end-declaration scoring; the original alternating-team turns are omitted.

Time Shift's generated statement families use a 24-hour clock: six digits and a single easy forward move, minute carry, backward borrowing, then seven digits, midnight crossings and two or three successive moves. Adjacent digit slots form a two-digit hour or minute; displayed move amounts are fixed clues. These are new practice sentences, not transcriptions of broadcast questions. All valid arrangements are accepted, including alternatives to the generation witness. Pixel Art validates every possible rotation of the chosen three cards instead of requiring a fixed witness. Stages 1–5 retain their target coverage requirements, while stages 6–8 require denser mixed targets and add similar distractors. Block Count retains the original stage 1–5 dimensions and extends to 5×5, 6×6 and 7×7 in stages 6–8.

## Verification

`tests/arena-puzzles.cjs` checks generated balance solutions, exact 1–6/1–7 consumption and clock rollovers, independently computed pixel rotations and rejection of every same-color overlap, solid stack layers and SVG cube counts, and independent geometric matching of arrow rotations. Controller checks cover five/eight-stage completion, same-level retries, deadline and cooldown boundaries, card return, duplicate pattern answers, early end penalties and the six-round board replacement.

`tests/arena-theme.cjs` checks the hub and every catalog game for the shared final stylesheet and consistent folder navigation. Existing Genius tests additionally check that random A–P labels remain fixed throughout a board's memorization, hidden-answer and review phases, and that solution coordinates use the same labels.
